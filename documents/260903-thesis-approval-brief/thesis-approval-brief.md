# Thesis Direction Approval Brief

**Working title:** *Can LLM-Generated Hard Labels Replace Gold Training Labels for Compact Entity Resolution Models?*

**Purpose of this document:** Obtain supervisor feedback on the thesis question,
experimental scope, and remaining dataset/model choices before further paid
labeling and GPU experiments.

## 1. Proposed thesis in brief

Entity Resolution (ER) determines whether two records refer to the same
real-world entity. Supervised ER systems normally require benchmark gold labels,
which can be expensive to create for a new domain. A powerful LLM could instead
label the training pairs, after which a smaller cross-encoder model could be
trained for repeated, lower-cost inference.

This thesis asks:

> Can LLM-generated hard labels provide a practical alternative to benchmark
> gold training labels for compact cross-encoder Entity Resolution models while
> reducing deployment cost compared with direct LLM matching?

The study does not propose a new ER architecture. Its contribution is a
controlled empirical evaluation of an increasingly practical supervision
strategy: use a strong LLM once as a machine labeler, then deploy a compact
model.

## 2. Research questions

1. **Label utility:** How closely does a compact model trained on LLM-generated
   hard labels match the performance of the same model trained on benchmark
   gold labels?
2. **Robustness:** Is the relationship consistent across different ER domains
   and compact cross-encoder architectures?
3. **Economics:** How much does machine labeling cost, and after how many
   inference queries does the trained compact model become cheaper than direct
   LLM matching?

The primary metric is match-class F1. Precision, recall, macro F1, accuracy,
training and inference time, throughput, labeling cost, inference cost, and the
break-even query count are supporting measurements.

## 3. Proposed experimental design

The final experiment is a predeclared:

- **3 benchmark datasets** × **3 compact cross-encoder models** × **2 training
  label sources** = **18 train/evaluate cells**.
- The two label sources are the official benchmark gold training labels and
  hard labels generated by GPT-5.6 Sol with high reasoning through OpenRouter.
- Each dataset also receives one direct-LLM baseline, producing three direct
  accuracy/cost baselines in total.
- The same model configuration is used for the gold and LLM-label arms; only
  the training target source changes.
- One predeclared run is performed per cell. Results are not used to select
  favorable reruns or tune one label-source arm differently.
- Validation data is used during development under a frozen protocol. The
  official test splits remain locked until datasets, models, prompts, metrics,
  and evaluation IDs are finalized.

Gold labels and entity identifiers are never sent to the LLM. Machine labeling
uses training pairs only. Missing, duplicate, malformed, or extra labels cause
publication to fail rather than being silently repaired.

## 4. Completed pilot evidence

The first vertical slice uses WDC Products, Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B, and the two
training-label sources.

### Machine labeling

- A fixed random sample of 300 WDC training pairs compared three GPT-5.6 Sol
  reasoning settings against gold labels.
- Sol-high was selected for its cost/accuracy balance: 291/300 correct at
  USD 0.327135.
- The complete 2,500-row WDC training split was labeled for USD 2.693225.
- LLM and gold training labels agreed on 2,421/2,500 pairs (96.84%).

### Compact-model validation result

| Training labels | Match F1 | Macro F1 | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Benchmark gold | 0.82084 | 0.88533 | 0.9216 |
| GPT-5.6 Sol-high hard labels | 0.81413 | 0.88158 | 0.9200 |

For this one dataset-model combination, the LLM-hard arm was 0.00671 lower in
match F1 (about 0.67 percentage points). This is promising evidence that the
machine labels may be practically useful, but it is not yet a thesis-level
conclusion: the remaining datasets, models, direct-LLM baselines, and locked
test evaluation are required.

## 5. Current second-dataset status

DBLP-ACM has been integrated as the second dataset to add a scholarly-record
domain distinct from product matching.

- Official structured split sizes were independently verified: 7,417 training,
  2,473 validation, and 2,473 locked test pairs.
- Dataset preparation is deterministic and source-verified.
- The complete offline labeling, target-publication, validation, and Qwen
  execution workflow has passed using a fake zero-cost client.
- No production DBLP labels, trained DBLP model, validation predictions, or
  test predictions currently exist.

DBLP-ACM is an older, established benchmark. This makes it useful for comparison
with prior ER work, but benchmark age and possible exposure in LLM/model
pretraining must be reported as contamination risks. Claims should therefore be
about performance on established benchmarks, not guaranteed performance on
novel contemporary data.

## 6. Expected contributions

1. A controlled multi-dataset, multi-model comparison of gold versus
   LLM-generated training labels for compact ER cross-encoders.
2. A reproducible full-label pipeline with blinded prompts, complete-coverage
   checks, failure recovery, provenance, and independently validated targets.
3. An accuracy-and-cost comparison between gold-supervised compact models,
   LLM-supervised compact models, and direct LLM matching.
4. Empirical guidance on when one-time LLM machine labeling is a practical
   alternative to human/benchmark supervision.

## 7. Scope and limitations

The study intentionally excludes active learning, low-label budgets, rationale
distillation, repeated-run selection, and new model architectures. These would
create additional research questions and weaken the focus of the comparison.

Important limitations are:

- One run per cell limits estimation of random-seed variance. Breadth across
  datasets and architectures is used as the principal robustness check.
- Public benchmarks may have appeared in LLM or pretrained-model data; exposure
  is often undisclosed and cannot be ruled out completely.
- LLM labels are not human annotations and may reproduce systematic labeler
  biases.
- Results from three benchmarks cannot establish universal ER performance.
- Cost conclusions depend on a dated provider-pricing snapshot and must include
  sensitivity analysis when prices change.
